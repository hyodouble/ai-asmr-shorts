## Review (2026-11-14, Instagram drop)

Method: first frame (0.3 s) of 10 high and 10 low reels per channel, side by side, plus the per-reel numbers above.

**Tofu.** 13 of 19 reels reached 1,000 up to #20; 2 of 13 did from #21 on. The break matches a change in the opening caption.
- High reels open with a full sentence about the animal's trouble: "She was so cold...", "She couldn't cross the stream...", "Her dam broke...", "She was so hungry...".
- Low reels (#21–#32) open with an exclamation: "Oops... stuck!", "Poof!", "Whoa!", "Too high!", "Missed again...".
- The two later hits (#26 "Too big!", #28 "Help!") show the trouble in the frame itself: a snowball bigger than the badger, a lynx stuck up a tree.
- Guess, not proven: the sentence tells a stranger who to feel for in the first second. The setting did not change between the two groups, so the snow park is not the cause.

**Mochi.** Helper stories (#23 on) hit 1,000 in 6 of 16; before that 4 of 22. The pattern inside the helper stories is weaker than Tofu's.
- Hits #24, #25, #26, #28 open with the visitor already in trouble in front and Mochi asleep behind ("Stuck in the jar", "flipped"). #27 has the same opening and still got 125.
- Low reels often open on a state, not a problem: "Hic!", "Wide awake!", "Achoo!", "POP?!".

**Change next (test for 6 reels per channel, then compare):**
1. Tofu: first caption is a full "She/He ..." sentence naming the trouble. No exclamation openers.
2. Both: the trouble is visible in the first frame (animal stuck, flipped, too small next to the thing).
3. YouTube stays at 3–5 views per short after 60+ uploads. Uploading costs effort and returns nothing; stop or change format is the owner's call.
